Introduction

The Sandhya Yantra is a precision astronomical instrument designed to track the trajectory of solar shadows throughout the year. Based on the principles of the ancient gnomon, it serves as a physical manifestation of the relationship between the Earth's rotation and its orbit around the Sun.

By analyzing the length and orientation of the shadow cast by a vertical pillar (the gnomon), we can derive critical astronomical data including solar altitude, azimuth, and the time of day. This project aims to digitize these geometric relationships, providing an educational bridge between historical observation and modern physics.

Working Principle

☀️

Solar Altitude

The angle of the Sun above the horizon. As the altitude increases, the shadow length decreases linearly according to the cotangent of the angle.

🧭

Azimuth Direction

The horizontal angle of the Sun. The shadow always points exactly opposite to the Sun's azimuth, allowing for cardinal direction mapping.

⏱️

Time Calibration

By observing the shadow's rotation around the gnomon, solar time can be determined relative to the solar noon (shortest shadow).

The Physics of Shadows

The geometry of the Sandhya Yantra is governed by the right-angle triangle formed by the gnomon height, the shadow length, and the solar ray.

L = h / tan(α)
L = h × cot(α)

Where:

  • L: Shadow Length
  • h: Gnomon Height
  • α: Solar Altitude Angle

Architecture & Design

Premium Materials

To ensure thermal stability and precision, the instrument uses materials with low coefficients of expansion. The base is constructed from polished white marble to provide a high-contrast background for the shadow, while the gnomon is fashioned from anodized aerospace-grade aluminum.

Geometric Precision

The structure is designed around a perfect 90-degree vertical axis. The circular base is calibrated with degree markings every 1° to allow for the precise measurement of the solar azimuth throughout the day.

Implementation Process

The methodology involves a three-stage approach: mathematical derivation of the shadow equations, physical prototyping for scale verification, and finally, digital simulation to model year-round trajectories.

Overview

The Sandhya Yantra project investigates the relationship between solar position and shadow geometry, providing a functional tool for astronomical education.

Utilizes a vertical gnomon to cast shadows on a calibrated plane, enabling the calculation of time and solar angle.

Physics
